# foot_pipeline — the server side of the football project

Everything that runs on the o2switch server (`~/data/foot_3d`), up to the sources of MotherDuck:

| Step | Code | Cron |
|---|---|---|
| FotMob calendars of the current season | `pmd/fixtures.py` | every day at 9:05 |
| Download and compute each match, 4 h after kick-off | `tools/run_missing.py` (pipeline `pmd/`) | every 15 min |
| 3D portrait of the match on data-workers.com | `tools/publish_web.py`, `fabric/`, `web/` | after each match |
| BigQuery `foot_new` | `tools/load_bigquery.py` | every 15 min |
| MotherDuck sources (`md:foot`, schema `raw`), then dbt build of [foot_db](https://github.com/krosamont/foot_db) | `tools/to_bronze.py`, `tools/lake_update.sh` | every 15 min |
| Check that every match played is everywhere (e-mail) | `tools/check_matches.py` | 23:30 |
| Old project seasons 2020-2021 to 2024-2025 (Parquet) -> MotherDuck `md:foot`, schema `raw_legacy` | `tools/load_legacy.py` | once (2026-10-27) |

The full crontab is in `deploy/crontab.txt`. Data, downloads, generated files and environments stay on the server
(`.gitignore`). The bronze / silver / gold models are in the separate repository foot_db.

# France – England · match fabric (clone of wc26.bogachev.fr)

Generative 3D data portrait of the match built from `data/France_vs_England/France_vs_England_qualifiers_expanded.csv` (WhoScored/Opta events with xG)
and rendered with Three.js. Every visual element is driven by the event data; there is no procedural noise.

## Pipeline

```
python3 tools/build_data.py data/France_vs_England/France_vs_England_qualifiers_expanded.csv   # -> fabric/data.js
python3 tools/reference/fit_camera.py   # pitch corners measured on the reference video -> fabric/camera.js
python3 tools/serve.py                  # then open http://localhost:8000/fabric/ (no-cache server)
node tools/render.js --fps 60 --out out/frames --scale 1           # headless Chrome frames (stops at the end)
tools/encode.sh out/frames out/fabric.mp4 60                       # H.264 video
tools/reference/sbs.sh out/fabric.mp4 out/sbs.mp4                  # reference | ours side by side
python3 tools/reference/compare.py out/cmp.png 4 14 22             # stills at given video seconds
python3 tools/reference/strip.py out/fabric.mp4 out/strip.png 0 4 0.3   # motion strips (reference top, ours bottom)
```

`tools/` needs `npm i puppeteer-core` (done in `tools/node_modules`) and Chrome for Testing in `~/.cache/puppeteer`.

## What maps to what

| Visual | Data |
|---|---|
| Two independent cloths | each team is its own full mesh anchored at its own goal line |
| Territory (front) | follows the CSV: every on-ball action (pass received = endX/endY, carry, recovery...) needs its side's line just past where it happened (3 m); when it is not there, that part of the line is pushed out to it (bulge, sigma 10 m). Ground is kept until the other side's actions take it back. Where the CSV moves the line a lot in little match time, the clock slows so the line moves at ~45 m per video second (video ~259 s). Measured: 90 % of the 1,501 actions sit on their side of the line when they happen; all 60 shots/goals/fouls are reached. |
| Half-time | during the break the camera swings 180 degrees round the pitch (eased, ~3 s); the second half is seen from the other side, so both teams now attack the other way on screen |
| Restarts | every goal: after the flood the front glides exactly onto the centre line and stays there, flat, for 30 match-seconds (slowed to ~1 s of video); the relief stays flat 25 s then regrows over 120 s. Second half: the front glides calmly back to the centre line during the break. |
| Fouls | the 22 fouls of the CSV (location + offender): the relief sinks flat (eased in video time); first part of the line runs out to the foul point for the fouled side (percée), then the whole line glides calmly to a flat straight line through the foul, holds 0.5 s, and play restarts from there. A white flash at the spot, a ring running out, the foul line lit across the pitch, a faint screen flash and a `FOUL · player` tag |
| Percées | a shot, goal or foul can only happen where the attacking side's line has got to: around the action's Y the front runs out to the action point (+3 m), holds, then falls back. Counter-attacks (FastBreak shots; fouls after >= 25 m gained in <= 15 s) make a narrow tongue (sigma 4 m), other actions a broader bulge (7 m). When the two sides' actions follow each other far apart, the clock slows so the line crosses at ~45 m/s. Verified: the line reaches all 60 shots/goals/fouls. |
| Who pushes | the side holding more territory (blended with momentum) (low-passed, with hysteresis) rides on top: broad body, ridge ~16 m behind its front, front dipping onto the opponent |
| Who is pushed | stays low and folds up into a lip where the front presses it |
| Hills | one per missed shot at its exact spot: a LOW hill (9 m) whose WIDTH grows with xG (7 m + 55 m x xG radius); only goals stand tall (spire 54 m). Tagged with player, outcome and xG from xG 0.15. All non-goal relief is softly capped at 14 m |
| Set pieces | every corner raises a wide dome (14 m) where the ball is sent and the attacking line runs out to it; every free kick raises one where it is sent (up to 11 m, higher near goal); every shot also lifts a wide base (up to 10 m) under its spike |
| Relief of advances | every on-ball action that moves a side up the pitch lifts the cloth where it lands: low (up to 5 m, more near the opponent goal) and WIDER with its xT (9 -> 23 m); it settles over ~0.7 s. Percée tips: 6 m, wider with xG |
| Goal | clock freeze, scorer spire (54 m, rounded) + full-pitch flood of the scorer's cloth with its relief intact, glass card, score; slow recede |
| Penalty | flat black pitch with white markings and a glowing box, then the scorer's cloth rolls out of the box as a decelerating wave |
| Pulse | FotMob per-minute momentum (Kapich xT momentum from the CSV as fallback); the same value drives the 3D front and 35% of the dominance. Goals, substitutions (⇄ chips) and cards (yellow/red) are marked, home above and away below, with the player's name flashing when it happens. The cursor dot takes the colour of the side pushing on the map. This match has no cards in either data source. |
| Half-time | at the whistle the line glides flat to the centre line (1.6 s), then the camera turns 180°; no foul restart between the whistle and the kick-off |
| Shots | every shot: a small hill and a tag (shooter · outcome · xG, 40 s); counter-attacks (FastBreak, or ≥ 25 m gained in ≤ 15 s): an 18 m breaking wave on the attacker's cloth along its run |
| Fonts | `assets/fonts/fonts.css`: Inter Black for numbers and team names, Poppins for all text (both bundled, Helvetica as last fallback); works on a server |
| Sky | takes the colour of the dominant side (territory + momentum), the more so the wider the gap (full colour at a dominance of 0.3); navy during the penalty, dims at full time |
| Comment lire | the link at the top right opens a French reading guide at once: one generic picture for every match, `assets/howto/comment-lire.jpg` (Équipe A in blue vs Équipe B in red, 1-0, a shot peak, notes with leader lines, the data sources). It is made by `node tools/make_howto.js` (an anonymised real frame + the notes of `fabric/js/howto.js`); rerun it after changing the notes. Esc or ✕ closes it. |
| Teams | name (`team_name`), logo (`team_logo`) and colours come from `<match>_match_info.csv`; cloth colour = FotMob dark-mode colour, HSL lightness clamped to 0.45–0.93, away side switched to its other colour (or white / red / yellow) when the two are closer than ΔE 25. The same colours are used for the pulse chart, markers, scoreboard and cards. |
| Dramatic time | 0.50 s per minute + (0.12 + 0.7 x xG) s per shot, 3.5 s per goal, 8 s for the penalty, +1 s after each goal kick-off, +1.2 s for half-time, +0.7 s per foul, +1.6 s per card, plus slow-downs wherever the line must travel far (video ~259 s) |

Gain parameters were fitted against the reference (mean territory error 0.144 with the user's rules): `scratch/area.py` measures the blue/white share of every
reference frame, `window.__seamTrace` + `window.__share` replay the front without rendering, `scratch/fit.js`
runs a coordinate search (mean territory error 0.162 -> 0.116).

The 3D engine is split into ES modules in `fabric/js/` (entry `main.js`); all tunables are in `P` in `fabric/js/config.js` (override with `window.PARAMS` before loading).

| Module | Role |
|---|---|
| `config.js` | match data (`window.MATCH`), tunables `P`, math helpers, cloth grid size |
| `timeline.js` | periods (halves + extra time), match clock, goal stops, cards, slow windows, match time ↔ video time |
| `scene.js` | Three.js renderer, camera orbit, team palette, the two cloths (geometry + shaders), pitch lines |
| `engine.js` | the front line: CSV keyframes, front clock, restarts / resets, percées, goal build-up floor, one-way movement |
| `relief.js` | relief: battle body, shot hills, advances, set pieces, counter-attack waves, goal spires, penalty reveal |
| `sim.js` | one simulation step + geometry update (camera turn at every break) |
| `sky.js`, `pulse.js`, `cardfx.js`, `ui.js`, `fonts.js` | background, momentum bar, card effects, overlay UI, font stacks |
| `main.js` | frame loop, seeking / scrub, play-pause, test hooks used by `tools/*.js`, ready signal |

## Installation (Mac or server)

```
./setup.sh                 # .venv (requirements.txt), Node packages, Chrome for Testing 131, ffmpeg check
./setup.sh --system        # + system packages (Linux: apt-get with Chrome's libraries and ffmpeg; macOS: Homebrew)
./setup.sh --check         # + the 10 engine rules on France-England
```
Headless Chrome is found by `tools/browser.js` (`$CHROME`, else the puppeteer cache, else a system Chrome). WebGL uses the GPU on macOS and software rendering (SwiftShader, no graphics card needed) elsewhere or with `GL=swiftshader`; both give the same picture. HTTPS certificates come from certifi (`pmd/config.py`), nothing to export by hand.

### Server with Docker

```
docker build --platform linux/amd64 -t match-fabric .
docker run --rm --platform linux/amd64 -v "$PWD/out:/app/out" -v "$PWD/data:/app/data" -v "$PWD/output:/app/output" match-fabric --fotmob 6106274
```
The image (Python 3.12, Node, ffmpeg, Chrome for Testing for x86-64 Linux) runs `python -m pmd.make3d`; the video lands in `out/`. On a plain Debian / Ubuntu server without Docker: `./setup.sh --system`, then `.venv/bin/python -m pmd.make3d --fotmob <id>`.

## One command, from a FotMob id to the video

```
python -m pmd.make3d --fotmob 6106254                 # WhoScored id looked up in data/fixtures/whoscored_fixtures.csv
python -m pmd.make3d --fotmob 4737562 --whoscored 1891177 --no-render
```
Steps: WhoScored id → pipeline (downloads cached in `output/<fotmob id>/raw`) → exports copied to `data/<match>/` → `validate_events.py` → `build_data.py` (`fabric/data/<date>_<home>_vs_<away>.js`) → `check_engine.js --match <match>` → `render.js` + ffmpeg → `out/<match>_3d.mp4`. It stops at the first failed check (`--force` to go on). Open one match in the browser with `fabric/index.html?match=<date>_<home>_vs_<away>` (e.g. `?match=2026-10-14_roma_vs_real-madrid`; the name is printed by `build_data.py`) (`python tools/serve.py`).

Extra time is drawn (two more periods, minutes 91–120, the map turns at every break); penalty shoot-outs are not drawn.


## Momentum, xT, cards, data checks

- xT and momentum are recomputed in the export script (AKapich's method): xT of passes and carries on a 12 x 8 grid, momentum = per-minute best xT per team (clipped 0..0.1), 4-minute window, decay 0.25, gaussian sigma 1, home minus away. Exported as `<match>_momentum.csv`; the 3D uses it before FotMob.
- Cards: small yellow / red card on the momentum bar; a full-screen effect (card dropping in with a glow; red: the screen pulses red; yellow: a rain shower). This match has no card; `?demoCards=1` previews two made-up cards labelled DÉMO.
- `tools/validate_events.py <csv> [--momentum ...] [--fotmob ...]`: data consistency rules for any match (pitch bounds, foul pairs and possession, shot halves, pass ends, period markers, time order, carries, cards vs FotMob, xT sign, momentum vs FotMob).

## Another match

1. `python -m pmd.run --fotmob <id> --whoscored <id> --out data/<Match> --cache data/<Match>/raw` (or set the ids at the top of `postmatchdashboard_whoscored_v2.py`): writes `<Home>_vs_<Away>_qualifiers_expanded.csv`, `_momentum.csv`, `_match_info.csv`, `_general.json`, `_Dashboard.png`.
2. `python tools/validate_events.py <Home>_vs_<Away>_qualifiers_expanded.csv --momentum <Home>_vs_<Away>_momentum.csv [--fotmob matchDetails.json]` — data rules (pitch bounds, foul pairs and possession, shots, pass ends, periods, carries, cards vs FotMob, xT, momentum, extra time, own goals).
3. `python tools/build_data.py <Home>_vs_<Away>_qualifiers_expanded.csv [--home "Home name"]` — builds `fabric/data.js` (home side = first 'Start' event by default; 3-letter codes, minutes, own goals handled).
4. `node tools/check_engine.js` — the engine rules on the simulation (actions reached, line at fouls, CSV placement, flat kick-offs, no step back while the scorer has the ball before a goal (CSV possession, until the flood), team names / logos / colours from match_info, no stray hills, speed, one-way movement). Exit code 1 if a rule fails.
5. Player cards (photo, age, club or country, shirt) come from `<match>_players.csv` (FotMob line-ups, written by the export). `build_data.py` downloads the player photos and team logos once into `assets/media/` (3 tries; an image that cannot be fetched keeps its FotMob URL) and `render.js` waits until every image is loaded before the first frame, so a network hiccup can no longer leave a card without its photo. Optional input: FotMob momentum (`assets/momentum_fotmob.json`, only used when the CSV momentum is missing). Not supported yet: extra time and penalty shoot-outs (the validator warns).


## FotMob ids, photos and match information

- The export adds `fotmob_player_id` to every event that has a player (matched on the FotMob line-ups: team + shirt number, then full name, then last name, accents ignored).
- Player photo: `https://images.fotmob.com/image_resources/playerimages/[id_joueur_fotmob].png` (e.g. Declan Rice, id 654096: https://images.fotmob.com/image_resources/playerimages/654096.png).
- Team logo: `https://images.fotmob.com/image_resources/logo/teamlogo/[fotmob_team_id].png` (`_small.png` for the small one).
- `<Home>_vs_<Away>_players.csv`: one row per player of the FotMob line-ups — fotmob_player_id, name, side, team_name, role (starter / sub), shirt, age, country, club (national teams only), photo (FotMob URL).
- `<Home>_vs_<Away>_match_info.csv`: one row per team — matchId, side, team_name, fotmob_team_id, score, team_logo, team_logo_small, color_lightMode, color_darkMode, font_lightMode, font_darkMode, matchTimeUTC, matchTimeUTCDate, leagueName, leagueRoundName, gender, stadium, city, country, lat, long, capacity, surface, attendance, referee.


## WhoScored match ids (`data/fixtures/sources/whoscored*.csv`)

- These files were made by hand with the **Instant Data Scraper** browser extension, run on WhoScored tournament
  fixtures pages such as https://www.whoscored.com/regions/250/tournaments/12/europe-champions-league (one page per
  competition, scrolling through the weeks of the season). Two layouts come out of it and both are read:
  one row per day (day header + the matches of that day) and one row per match (no day: the date is then taken from
  FotMob, see `date_source`).
- `python -m pmd.whoscored_fixtures data/fixtures/sources/*.csv` merges them, extracts id / date / teams /
  competition / season / score from the match links, links each match to its FotMob id (same competition and season, same day +-1, same teams; without a usable day:
  the two teams within the season, the score deciding when a fixture was played twice; club-name aliases such as
  Copenhagen / København; the FotMob calendars of the seasons found are fetched automatically) and writes `fotmob_without_whoscored.csv` with the matches still missing (empty for 2026/2027).
- Limits of the manual export: the extension can cut a long day (18 Champions League matches at the same time came out
  as 9), the kick-off time cannot be tied to its match, and it has to be redone by hand for every season.

**Other seasons, automatically: `pmd/whoscored_schedule.py`.** It fetches the WhoScored schedule of whole league
seasons with `soccerdata` (`WhoScored(...).read_schedule()`, a real Chrome on WhoScored's fixtures pages), fetches the
FotMob calendar of the same season and links both with the rules above:

    python -m pmd.whoscored_schedule --season 2025/2026                      (Ligue 1, LaLiga, Serie A, Bundesliga, Premier League)
    python -m pmd.whoscored_schedule --season 2025/2026 --leagues laliga --out data/fixtures

-> `whoscored_schedule_<season>.csv` (same columns as above + `kickoff_utc`) and `fotmob_without_whoscored_<season>.csv`.
Tested on LaLiga 2025/2026: 380/380 WhoScored matches linked to FotMob, 380/380 identical scores, no FotMob match left
(`data/fixtures/whoscored_schedule_2025-2026_laliga.csv`). About 3 minutes per league season; soccerdata caches the
pages in `~/soccerdata` (or `$SOCCERDATA_DIR`). The Champions League is not one of soccerdata's WhoScored defaults
(it needs a custom entry in soccerdata's `league_dict.json`, not done yet): for it, keep the manual export.
